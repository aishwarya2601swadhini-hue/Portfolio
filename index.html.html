SEG. 01 NIVIA

Wear the Blue. Own the Blue.

SPORTS STRATEGY GO-TO-MARKET BRAND CULTURE

What if a jersey could become more than something you wear?

This was a full strategy & creative deck exploring how Nivia could build a stronger cultural connection around India's football jersey. Rather than positioning the jersey simply as merchandise, the idea was to make it a visible expression of identity, pride and belonging among Indian football fans — covering market opportunity, consumer segmentation, positioning, culture, amplification, distribution and measurement.

157.2MUnique ISL viewers, 2024–25 — up 18% YoY
DiscontinuedNike halted retail sale of the official India jersey for years
₹150 Cr+Indian cricket jersey sales during the 2023 World Cup — 5x normal demand

The gap was never passion. It's that no one built the moment and the access for football fans — that's the demand Nivia can create.

POSITIONING

Visible Belonging.

For the Indian football fan whose passion has no visible home — Nivia is the everyday jersey that turns individual support into a shared, visible movement. Worn together, blue stops being a shirt and becomes a statement.

SEG. 02 VYBE

Your routine. Your way.

AI PRODUCT FITNESS CONSUMER EXPERIENCE

What if your fitness app adapted to your life?

VYBE was an AI-in-business concept built around a simple observation: everyone's routine looks different. Instead of asking users to fit themselves into a predefined fitness plan, VYBE gives them the flexibility to create a routine that works for them — building their own activity blocks, using templates, planning their week and tracking different aspects of their wellness journey.

THE IDEA

Start with what works for you.

Build around: Workouts · Steps · Water · Sleep · Cardio · Meditation · Custom Activities

Your routine should fit your life — not the other way around.

SEG. 04 ECONOMICS NEWSLETTER

The ₹200 Lamp That Stole Instagram

ECONOMICS DIGITAL COMMERCE CONSUMER BEHAVIOUR

MU TIMES · SPECIAL EDITION · CO-AUTHORED WITH ANSH JAIN, RISHIKA & PURABB BHARTIA

How dropshipping made everyone a CEO.

It's 1 AM in Delhi. A Reel shows a warm, golden-hour "Sunset Lamp" — originally ₹2,999, on flash sale tonight for ₹1,299, Cash on Delivery. Seven days later, a lightweight box arrives from a "luxury aesthetic brand" that owns no warehouse and operates out of a college hostel room. The founder bought that exact lamp for ₹180 from a wholesaler in Surat on IndiaMART, and never touched the parcel before it reached the buyer's hands. The newsletter used this one transaction — and a framework we called "Crossonomics" — to unpack how fixed costs, information asymmetry and willingness to pay shape what we pay online.
